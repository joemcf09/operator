================================================================================
  OPERATOR - CHARTER: BROKERS, CLIENTS AND TRIP TYPES
  Reference for the private charter line of business
================================================================================

  Brokers and competing operators are real companies, named and shown with their
  own marks to identify who places the work and who you lose it to. Their
  appearance implies no affiliation or endorsement, and no trademark rights are
  transferred. Sources for every mark are listed in the program under
  System > Credits. Clients are generic types, not real people or
  organisations: naming actual charter clients would be inappropriate.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  BROKERS - who brings you the work
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Every enquiry arrives through one of these. The 'fussy' figure is how hard
  they are to win on price: above 1.00 means they push back harder on a high
  quote, below 1.00 means they are more price-led and easier to undercut.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CLIENT TYPES - who is actually on board
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Each type pays differently and, more importantly, judges the trip
  differently. The percentages are how much of their satisfaction score comes
  from each thing. Tolerance is how forgiving they are of a late departure:
  below 1.00 is less forgiving than average.

  Corporate group
      a board party travelling to a site visit
      judges you on: punctuality 45% - a smooth flight 30% - the right cabin 25%
      pays 1.00x the going rate - lateness tolerance 1.00

  Private owner
      a family trip with luggage and a dog

      pays 1.05x the going rate - lateness tolerance 0.90

  Investment fund

      judges you on: punctuality 60% - a smooth flight 25% - the right cabin 15%
      pays 1.15x the going rate - lateness tolerance 0.75

  Sports team

  Film production

  Government delegation

  Medical transfer

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIP TYPES - the shape of the work
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  One-way  -  pays about 1.00x a single sector
      A single sector. You position in empty and go home empty unless you can
      sell the leg.

  Day return  -  pays about 1.85x a single sector  (returns)
      Out in the morning, wait on the ground, home the same evening. The
      crew's day is long.

  Overnight  -  pays about 1.95x a single sector  (returns, 2 day(s))
      Out one day, back another. Crew hotel and per diem are yours to pay.

  Multi-city roadshow  -  pays about 2.90x a single sector  (returns, 3 day(s), 2 extra stop(s))
      Several cities in as few days as possible. Every sector counts and the
      schedule is unforgiving.

  Short notice  -  pays about 1.35x a single sector  (short notice)
      Wheels up within a day or two. Pays well because nobody else can cover
      it.

  Tour leg  -  pays about 1.50x a single sector  (1 day(s), 1 extra stop(s))
      One leg of a longer itinerary, handed to you because the regular
      operator is short.

  Group charter  -  pays about 1.25x a single sector
      More passengers than a light jet will take. Cabin size decides whether
      you can bid at all.

  Long range  -  pays about 1.15x a single sector  (1 day(s))
      Intercontinental, with customs at both ends and a crew close to their
      duty limit.

  Event peak  -  pays about 1.60x a single sector  (1 day(s), event surge)
      Everyone wants the same airport on the same day. Rates rise, slots are
      scarce.

  Medical transfer  -  pays about 1.50x a single sector  (short notice)
      A patient and escort, on a schedule set by a hospital rather than a
      diary.

  Empty leg  -  a discounted one-off
      When a trip leaves your aircraft away from base, a passenger may ask
      about the leg home at 22-45% of the normal rate.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  REQUIREMENTS - what a trip can demand
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Checked against your fleet before you can bid. Some block the quote
  outright; others are noted and cost you the client's goodwill.

  skis and boards in the baggage hold
      requires baggage volume - price effect 1.05x
  golf clubs for four
      requires baggage volume - price effect 1.00x
  a dog travelling in the cabin
      price effect 1.05x
  catering from a named restaurant
      price effect 1.10x
  customs and immigration on arrival
      requires customs and immigration - price effect 1.05x
  a cabin attendant throughout
      requires a cabin attendant - price effect 1.15x
  wi-fi that works over the ocean
      requires connectivity - price effect 1.10x
  a wheelchair and assistance at both ends
      price effect 1.05x
  press waiting at the destination
      price effect 1.10x
  discretion: no tail number published
      price effect 1.05x
  a short runway at the destination
      requires a short runway - price effect 1.10x
  an early departure before the noise curfew lifts
      requires a curfew departure - price effect 1.08x

  Always checked, whatever the trip asks for:
      seats against passenger count - cabin class against expectation
      runway length at every field on the itinerary
      range without a fuel stop on the longest sector
      a cabin attendant for eight or more passengers, or flights over five hours
      customs when the trip crosses a border
